Software Development

Even as a kid, I was fascinated by computers. Their speed, efficiency, and endless possibilities drew me in. I've always loved how they work, so when I discovered I could turn that passion into a career, I knew I had found my calling.

I got into coding early at a summer camp called "iD Tech" at Colorado State University. There, I made a simple video game mod, and from then on, I was hooked. My passion for computers just kept growing. In high school and college, I dove deeper into coding and explored all areas of Computer Science. I became especially interested in Systems Engineering and Software Development. Solving problems and helping others is what I find most rewarding.

I began working for the University of Wyoming's Classroom Technology Services (CTS) as a student field tech, and eventually promoted to a software developer. Fixing classroom technology across campus and developing tools for technicians became my primary focus. Before I joined CTS, the department had started building "Bronson," a web tool that centralizes only resources for student techs into one application. Seeing the development and the drive to fix recurring problems in the department inspired me to join the project, and it has been one of my most rewarding experiences.

My main contribution to Bronson was an integrated ticket response system that serves tickets directly to technicians so that they can go respond to help requests. CTS uses TeamDynamix to track maintenance requests, issues, and tutorials. Thus, "Tickex" was born: a tool for techs to view and respond to tickets quickly and remotely. It utilizes API calls, authentication, and uses intelligent algorithms for maximum efficiency. I'm proud that Tickex is now fully integrated, and techs will use it every day once Bronson launches within the next few months.

Primetime Paradox

For my senior capstone, my team and I made a video game called "Primetime Paradox" using Unity, Blender, FL Studio, and C#. It's a rogue-like rhythm game where you dungeon crawl to the beat, fight enemies, and collect items.

We wanted to solve a common problem in rhythm games: levels that lock players into one playing style. Our goal was to create a free-roam environment that encourages you to play to the beat. This project taught me a lot about game development, teamwork, and problem-solving.

We handled everything from the 3D models, music, sound, and all the programming. I was the lead sound engineer and back-end developer, making sure the game ran smoothly. I built the beat detection system from scratch so it could be used for game mechanics, animation, and events. Unity has been a great way to learn more about game development, and you can play it now on stitch!
