Software Development

Even as a kid, I was fascinated by computers. Their speed, efficiency, and endless possibilities have always caught my attention. I've always wanted to know how they work, so when I discovered I could turn that interest into a career, I knew I had found my calling.

I got into coding young at a summer camp called "iD Tech" at Colorado State University. There, I made a simple video game mod where I got to manipulate and write real code, and from then on, I was hooked. Throughout high school, my passion for computers just kept growing. I dove deeper into coding and started exploring all areas of Computer Science. I became especially interested in Systems Engineering and Software Development in college, and focused much of my time on algorithms and how back end structures work. I've built projects in C, C++, C#, Python, Java, Rust, and more, and I'm always looking for new languages and frameworks to learn.

At the University of Wyoming, I began working for the Classroom Technology Services (CTS) department as a student field tech, and eventually promoted to a software developer. My job was to fix and maintain classroom technology all across campus and develop web application monitoring tools for technicians. This web application was called "Bronson," and it was started by a few coworkers before I had been hired. Bronson is a web tool that centralizes online resources for student techs into one application. Seeing the development and the drive to fix recurring problems for our technicians and clients was what inspired me to join the project, and it has been one of my most fulfilling experiences so far in my career.

My main contribution to Bronson was building an integrated ticket response system that notifies technicians about fresh tickets so that they can respond to help requests. UW already has a service called TeamDynamix that tracks these ticket requests, issues, and tutorials. However, it is a clunky and slow system that is not optimized for CTS technicians to quickly view and respond to tickets. Thus, "Tickex" was born: a tool for techs to view and respond to tickets quickly and remotely. It utilizes API calls, proper authentication/authorization, and intelligent algorithms for maximum department efficiency. I'm proud to say that Tickex is now fully integrated, and techs will use it every day once Bronson launches within the next few months.


Primetime Paradox

At the University of Wyoming, I also had the opportunity to work on a senior capstone project with a team of four of my peers. For my senior capstone, my team and I made a video game called "Primetime Paradox" using Unity, Blender, FL Studio, and C#. It's a rogue-like rhythm game where you dungeon crawl to the beat, fight enemies, and collect items while trying to escape a dark cave.

We wanted to solve a common problem in rhythm games: music levels that lock players into one playing style. Our goal was to create a free-roam environment that encourages you to play to the beat, but still allows the player to play in their own way. This project taught me a lot about game development, teamwork, and problem-solving.

We made everything from the 3D models, music, sound, and all the programming by hand. For the project, I was the lead sound engineer and an additional back-end developer, making sure the game ran smoothly. I built our beat detection system from scratch so it could be used to sync game mechanics, animation, events, and more to the music. Unity has been a great way to learn more about game development, and you can play Primetime Paradox now on itch.io!

Music & Audio Technology

I was obsessed with practicing and learning music, and always looked for new ways to challenge myself. As I grew older, I became interested in music theory and writing electronic music. Along with live performance, I also compose electronic music, DJ, and manage live audio for stage productions. I earned my Audio Technology Certificate from the University of Wyoming, focusing on music production, live audio, and stage logistics, which has been a huge help for my professional audio work at the University of Wyoming AV departments.

At UW, I held two AV jobs, the first was being an AV technician for the Student Technical Services (STS) department at the UW Union. Some of my responsibilities included setting up/running/tearing down stage productions, coordinating with clients, and managing event scheduling. My second job was for the Classroom Technology Services (CTS) department at the IT department, where I would help install and maintain classroom AV systems across the UW campus. In both jobs, I have handled many performances/events and quickly resolved technical issues in hundreds of rooms.
